Overview

Focus: hip-to-hand separation, a direct hand path, and driving through the ball instead of rolling over. Every station attacks one piece of the swing — hitters should leave each one able to say what they worked on.

Format: 8 stations in two groups of four, 2 hitters per station, 6 minutes per station. One hitter works for 3 minutes while her partner resets the ball (or tosses at Station 4) and watches for the key cue, then they switch. The whole team works the Group A stations (1–4) for 24 minutes, then moves to the Group B stations (5–8) for 24 minutes, so every hitter gets all 8 stations in 48 minutes.

Lefties mirror every setup — the Station 5 target becomes the shortstop and Station 8 drives to left field.

Whistle at 3 minutes for partners to switch and at 6 minutes to rotate (1 → 2 → 3 → 4 and 5 → 6 → 7 → 8). Four stations with 2 hitters each fits 8 hitters; with more, put 3 at a station and give each 2 minutes. Put a coach at Station 4 (front toss).

Equipment

  • 4 tees and 4 hitting nets — set for Group B, with one of each moved to Stations 2 and 4 for Group A
  • 2 PVC pipes about bat length (Stations 1 and 2) and 1 long, heavy PVC or weighted bat (Station 3)
  • 2 short one-hand bats (Stations 5 and 6)
  • L-screen and a bucket to sit on (Station 4)
  • 4 throw-down plates or markers and 4 cones (renumber them when you switch)
  • About 100 softballs in 4 buckets; pitching screen and helmets for BP

Group A · Stations 1–4Hips and lower half

1PVC hip separation~10 reps

Feel the hips start forward while the hands stay back — the stretch that stores power.

Top view · right-handed hitter · pitcher at the top. Lefties mirror it.

  1. Set the pipeStand the PVC upright just behind the back foot. Grip the top with both hands at load height.
  2. Stride and fire the hipsStep toward the pitcher and turn the belt buckle forward while the hands stay on the pipe.
  3. Feel the stretch, then resetHold the stretched position for 2 seconds, then reset. Hips lead, hands lag.

Key cueHips go, hands stay.

Setup
Stand a PVC pipe upright just behind the back foot. The hitter grips the top of it with both hands, as if at the top of the load.
Reps
3 minutes per hitter — about 10 reps. Stride, fire the hips, hold the stretched position for 2 seconds, reset.
Coach it
Belt buckle turns toward the pitcher; hands do not leave the pipe; back heel comes up as the hips turn.
Watch for
Shoulders spinning open with the hips, or the hitter pulling the pipe forward — that means the hands are going with the hips.

2PVC behind the back10–12 turns

Prove the hips can deliver the turn on their own — the PVC, driven only by hip rotation, knocks the softball off the tee.

Top view · right-handed hitter · pitcher at the top. Lefties mirror it.

  1. Set upPVC across the lower back at belt height; tee at hip height where the back end of the pipe lands.
  2. Rotate the hipsFrom stance, fire the back hip. The back end of the PVC sweeps into the zone.
  3. Hit the ball with the pipeSquare the belt buckle to the pitcher; the PVC knocks the ball off the tee.

Key cueTurn the hips, hit the ball with the pipe.

Setup
PVC pipe held across the lower back at belt height. Set a tee at hip height out in front, where the back end of the pipe lands when the hips finish their turn. Adjust the tee once or twice until a good turn reaches it.
Reps
3 minutes per hitter — about 10–12 hip turns. Each rep starts from a normal stance and stride; the partner resets the ball on the tee.
Coach it
Fire the back hip; belt buckle finishes square to the pitcher; head stays still; the back end of the PVC is what hits the ball.
Watch for
Swinging the pipe with the arms or shoulders, leaning forward to reach the tee, or the back foot staying flat (hips stall).

3Power swings with long PVC8–10 swings

Keep the hips back and drive the hands through the ball while swinging something longer and heavier than a bat.

Top view · right-handed hitter · pitcher at the top. Lefties mirror it.

  1. Load and strideHold the long PVC like a bat; normal load and stride. Keep weight on the back hip.
  2. Hips stay backStart rotation without lunging; head and hips stay behind the front foot.
  3. Hands throughDrive the hands through the contact zone to full extension. The length punishes casting.

Key cueStay back, swing long through the zone.

Setup
A longer, heavier PVC pipe (or weighted training bat). Dry swings only — no ball. Space hitters well apart; nobody stands within swinging distance.
Reps
3 minutes per hitter — about 8–10 full, controlled swings with a breath between each. Quality over speed — the weight should slow the swing down so flaws show up.
Coach it
Load and stride like a normal swing; head and hips stay behind the front foot; hands stay close and then drive through to full extension.
Watch for
Casting (the pipe swings wide early), lunging forward onto the front foot, or muscling the pipe with the arms instead of turning.

4Front toss~15 tosses

Carry the hip work from Group A into a full swing on a moving ball.

Top view · right-handed hitter · pitcher at the top. Lefties mirror it.

  1. Set upTosser behind an L-screen out in front. Firm underhand toss to the middle of the plate.
  2. Load and separateStride and let the hips start while the hands stay back — the Station 1 feel.
  3. Hands throughDrive the hands through to extension; line drive up the middle.

Key cueHips first, then hands through.

Setup
L-screen about 15–20 feet in front of the plate. The tosser kneels or sits behind it and tosses firm, underhand strikes to the middle of the plate. Hit into a large net, or out to the field if there's room.
Reps
3 minutes per hitter — about 15 tosses. A coach tosses if possible; otherwise the partner tosses from behind the screen.
Coach it
Same stride and hip start as Station 1 (hips go, hands stay), then the hands drive through to extension for a line drive up the middle.
Watch for
Hips and hands firing together (no separation), lunging at the toss, or the tosser stepping out from behind the screen.

Group B · Stations 5–8Hands and bat path

5Top hand12–15 swings

A short, direct bat path with the top hand, producing line drives toward the second baseman.

Top view · right-handed hitter · pitcher at the top. Lefties mirror it.

  1. Set upShort bat in the top hand only; choke up and tuck the bottom hand to the chest.
  2. No legsFeet set, no stride, no hip turn. This drill isolates the bat path.
  3. Palm up through contactKnob to the ball, palm up through contact, line drive at the second baseman.

Key cueShort and direct: line drive to 2B.

Setup
Tee at the front of the plate, middle height. Short one-hand bat in the top hand; bottom hand tucked to the chest. Feet set — no stride, no hip turn.
Reps
3 minutes per hitter — about 12–15 swings off the tee; the partner resets the ball.
Coach it
Knob leads to the ball; palm up through contact; finish short. The ball should come off as a line drive at the 2B spot on the net.
Watch for
The barrel dropping and lofting the ball, the wrist rolling over early (ground ball to the pull side), or sneaking in a stride.

6Bottom hand12–15 swings

The bottom hand takes the knob straight to the ball and drives on to extension without peeling off.

Top view · right-handed hitter · pitcher at the top. Lefties mirror it.

  1. Set upShort bat in the bottom hand only; top hand on the chest or back hip.
  2. Hand path to the ballLead with the knob straight to the ball — no looping or sweeping out.
  3. Extend, don't peelDrive through to extension toward the pitcher. Front shoulder stays in; don't peel off.

Key cueStraight to it, then through it.

Setup
Tee at the front of the plate, middle height. Short one-hand bat in the bottom (lead) hand; top hand on the chest or back hip.
Reps
3 minutes per hitter — about 12–15 swings off the tee; the partner resets the ball.
Coach it
Knob goes straight to the ball; drive through to extension toward the pitcher; front shoulder stays closed until after contact.
Watch for
The lead arm sweeping out wide, the front shoulder flying open early (“peeling off”), or chopping down at the ball.

7Split grip half swings12–15 swings

Drive the barrel through the softball instead of rolling the wrists over.

Top view · right-handed hitter · pitcher at the top. Lefties mirror it.

  1. Split the gripSlide the hands a few inches apart on the handle. Choke up slightly for control.
  2. Half swingFrom load, swing to contact and a short finish. Stop before the wrists roll.
  3. Drive throughTop hand palm up, bottom hand palm down. The barrel drives through the ball.

Key cueDrive it, don't roll it.

Setup
Tee at the front of the plate, middle height. Regular bat with the hands split a few inches apart on the handle.
Reps
3 minutes per hitter — about 12–15 half swings off the tee; the partner resets the ball.
Coach it
Swing only to contact and a short finish; at contact the top hand is palm up and the bottom hand palm down; the split grip lets the hitter feel the barrel push through the ball.
Watch for
Top hand flipping over at contact (ground balls to the pull side) or the half swing turning into a full swing.

8Way outside, stay back12–15 swings

Let the outside pitch travel deep and drive it to the opposite field instead of reaching and rolling over.

Top view · right-handed hitter · pitcher at the top. Lefties mirror it.

  1. Set the teeTee just off the outside corner and deep, even with the back half of the plate.
  2. Stay backLet the ball travel. Hands stay inside; don't reach or lunge out over the plate.
  3. Drive oppoBarrel trails the hands; drive a line drive into the opposite-field gap.

Key cueLet it get deep, hit it where it's pitched.

Setup
Move the tee just off the outside corner and back, even with the back half of the plate (deep in the zone). Net target is the opposite-field side.
Reps
3 minutes per hitter — about 12–15 swings off the tee; the partner resets the ball.
Coach it
Stay back and let the ball get deep; hands stay inside the ball; barrel trails the hands and the ball is driven on a line to the opposite field.
Watch for
Lunging or reaching out over the plate, pulling off with the front side, or rolling over to the pull side.

Coach notes

  • Rotate on the whistle. Whistle at 3 minutes for partners to switch and at 6 minutes to rotate; everyone moves together.
  • One cue per station. Use the key cue; too many cues at once slows hitters down.
  • Safety. Stations 1, 2 and 3 swing or turn with pipe — keep the waiting partner well out of range and spread these stations apart. At Station 4 the tosser stays behind the L-screen.
  • Quality over quantity. If form breaks down near the end of a set, stop and reset rather than finishing the count.
  • Connect it to live hitting. During BP, call out the station cues (“hips go, hands stay,” “drive it, don't roll it”) so hitters link the drills to their full swing.
